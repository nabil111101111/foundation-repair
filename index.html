How to tell if you need foundation repair

Most foundation problems announce themselves inside the house first. Doors and windows that stick or swing on their own, cracks running diagonally from the corners of door frames, floors that slope or bounce, and gaps opening between walls and ceilings are the classic early signs. Outside, look for stair-step cracks in brick, a chimney pulling away, and soil that has dropped away from the foundation line.

Not every crack is a repair bill. Hairline cracks in concrete are usually shrinkage from curing, and thin drywall cracks over doors are common in newer homes. The pattern matters more than any single crack: signs that cluster on one side of the house, keep growing, or arrive together usually mean the foundation itself is moving and should be measured, not just watched.

What are the first signs of foundation problems in a home?

The first signs are usually sticking doors and windows, diagonal cracks above door frames, and floors that feel sloped or springy. Outside, watch for stair-step cracks in brickwork and gaps where soil has pulled away from the foundation. One sign alone can be harmless. Several appearing together, or one side of the house showing all of them, means the foundation is moving.

When is a foundation crack serious?

A foundation crack is serious when it is wider than a quarter inch, runs horizontally, offsets so one side sits higher than the other, or keeps growing between seasons. Stair-step cracks through brick mortar with matching interior damage belong in the same category. Hairline vertical cracks in a poured wall are usually shrinkage. Measure and photograph yours, and have anything beyond hairline inspected.

Foundation repair methods compared

The main structural methods are piers, which support a settled foundation from stable soil or rock below; slabjacking or foam lifting, which raises a settled slab section from underneath; and crack injection, which seals a leaking or cracked wall. Wall anchors and carbon-fiber straps handle a different problem, basement walls bowing inward under soil pressure. Drainage work such as grading, gutters, and interior drain systems supports all of them by controlling the water that caused the damage.

Choosing between them is a diagnosis question, not a preference. Piers fix a foundation that has lost support; they do nothing for a slab section floating over a void, and injection will not stop a wall that is still moving. A method pitched before anyone has measured elevations and identified the cause is a sales script, and it is the most common reason homeowners pay for the same problem twice.

What is the best foundation repair method?

The best method is the one that matches the cause: piers for a foundation that has settled, slabjacking or foam for a sunken slab section, straps or anchors for a bowing wall, and drainage where water is driving the movement. There is no best method in the abstract. Any company that names a method before measuring your home is selling, not diagnosing.

How long does foundation repair last?

75 years or more is the expected service life of properly installed steel or helical piers, and most are treated as permanent because they bear on stable material below the problem soil. Slabjacking and injection are shorter-lived fixes whose lifespan depends on the soil staying stable. Warranties differ by company, so the warranty document, not the sales pitch, defines what lasting means for your job.

What foundation repair costs, and how to check a quote

Nationally, foundation repair is priced by the pier and by the scope, not by the house. Piers run $1,000-$2,500 each installed, and a typical structural repair totals $4,000-$12,000. Smaller work sits below that range: slabjacking at $500-$1,500 per section, foam lifting at $1,000-$2,500, and crack injection at $500-$2,500. Drainage systems with a sump run $2,000-$6,000. These are published market ranges, not quotes, and your soil, pier count, and access move your number inside them.

You can sanity-check any quote with pier-count logic. A single settling corner might need 4 to 6 piers; a full side of a house can take 12 to 20 or more. Multiply the pier count by the per-pier price and compare it to the total. If the maths does not roughly work, or the quote refuses to break the price down, that is the red flag, not the total itself.

How much does foundation repair cost?

$4,000-$12,000 is the typical total for a structural foundation repair, built from piers at $1,000-$2,500 each installed. Minor work costs less: crack injection runs $500-$2,500 and slabjacking $500-$1,500 per section. The total is driven by pier count, depth to stable soil, and access. Treat any price given before an in-person inspection and elevation measurements as a guess, not a quote.

How do I know if a foundation repair quote is fair?

A fair quote is itemized: it states the pier type, the pier count and locations, the per-pier price, and separates lift work, drainage, and permits into their own lines. Check the per-pier price against the published $1,000-$2,500 range and the pier count against your elevation map. Two or three itemized quotes, or one engineer's report, will expose an inflated quote quickly.

Foundation repair questions, answered

How much will I pay for foundation repair through FoundationMatch?

You pay the local company that does the work, at the price in its written quote, and most structural repairs land between $4,000-$12,000. FoundationMatch does not add a charge on top of the contractor's price for homeowners. Before you agree to anything, you should hold an itemized quote showing pier counts and per-pier prices, so the total is something you can verify.

How does FoundationMatch matching work?

You tell us your city and what your home is doing, and we pass the request to a foundation repair company serving your area. That company contacts you to schedule an inspection at your home. There is no obligation to hire them, and getting more than one quote is something we recommend in writing across this site, not something we discourage.

Is the foundation inspection really free?

The initial inspection arranged through FoundationMatch is free: a local company visits, measures your foundation, and gives you a written quote, and you owe nothing if you decline the work. What is not free is an independent structural engineer's assessment, which typically costs a few hundred dollars and is worth it when you want a diagnosis nobody profits from.

Do you guarantee the foundation work?

No. FoundationMatch does not perform or guarantee the work, and any site that implies otherwise about a matching service is blurring the truth. The guarantee that matters is the repair company's warranty, and it should be given to you in writing before you sign, stating what is covered, for how long, and whether it transfers to the next owner.

Does homeowners insurance pay for foundation repair?

Usually no. Standard homeowners policies exclude settlement, soil movement, and wear over time, which account for most foundation damage. Sudden events can be different: a burst pipe that undermines a footing sometimes triggers partial coverage. Because the answer lives in your policy's exclusions, most homeowners plan on paying for structural repair themselves and compare itemized quotes carefully before committing.

How fast can someone come out to look at my foundation?

Timing depends on the local company's schedule, but inspection requests are normally contacted within one business day and visited within the same week. Active water entry, a wall that is visibly moving, or a door that suddenly will not close are reasons to say so in your request, because companies triage urgent structural signs ahead of routine checks.
