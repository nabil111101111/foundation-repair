Calculator questions

How accurate is this foundation repair cost calculator?

It gives a planning range, not a quote. Figures come from the published market ranges used across this site (piers at $1,000–$2,500 installed, drainage at $2,000–$6,000), combined with standard pier spacing of about 5–7 feet. Your real number depends on measured movement, soil depth to stable bearing, and access — which is why the next step after a range is always a free in-person inspection.

Why does the city change the estimate?

The city changes the context, not the unit prices. Soil series and shrink–swell rating affect how many piers a wall typically needs and how deep they must go; housing age affects footing style. The calculator shows your city's measured soil and housing data alongside the range so you can see why one city's typical job differs from another's.

How many piers does a house need?

Most settling walls need piers every 5–7 feet along the affected length: a single corner often takes 4–6, one full side 8–14, and a whole house 15–25 or more. Enter the length of wall that has dropped and the calculator converts it at 6-foot spacing, then prices that count at the published per-pier range.

What does the calculator not include?

It does not include cosmetic repairs (drywall, brick repointing, door rehanging), permits, engineering reports, or landscaping restoration, and it cannot price interior piers or access problems. Treat the top of the range as your planning number and get itemised quotes — our cost guide shows the red flags to check in any quote.
