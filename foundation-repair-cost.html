Foundation Repair Cost: What It Runs and How to Check a Quote

Foundation repair cost averages $4,000 to $12,000 for a typical residential job, with individual piers at $1,000 to $2,500 each installed. Small repairs, a few piers under one settled corner, can come in below that band; whole-house jobs with fifteen or more piers run above it. The number that decides your total is pier count, which is why the honest answer to how much does foundation repair cost always starts with measurements, not averages.

This page breaks the average foundation repair cost down by method and by problem, shows the pier-count patterns typical houses follow, and then does the thing most cost pages skip: it shows you how to check a real quote against those patterns. FoundationMatch is a matching service, not a contractor. The ranges here are compiled from published national cost guides, so you can walk into any quote conversation with the maths already done.

Foundation repair cost by method

Foundation work is priced by the unit, and the unit is usually the pier. When a company quotes your job, it is multiplying a per-pier price by a pier count, then adding the extras your house needs: interior access, drainage, permits, restoration. That is why the foundation repair cost per pier is the most useful number on this page. If you know the band a pier should sit in and the count patterns a house like yours usually needs, you can rebuild any contractor's total on the back of an envelope and see whether it adds up.

Method choice is not a menu you pick from by price. The diagnosis picks the method: piers where soil has failed under a footing, foam or grout where a slab section has dropped, drainage where water is the driver, injection where a crack leaks but the wall is stable. Quotes that lead with the cheapest method rather than the matching method are solving the wrong problem, and the repair fails in a predictable way a few seasons later.

Within the pier band, three things decide where your price lands. Depth is the biggest: a pier that reaches stable soil at 12 feet costs less than one driven 30 feet through fill or soft clay, and the crew cannot know the final depth until the first piers go in, which is why honest quotes state the assumed depth and the rate if piers run deeper. Access is second: interior piers mean cutting and later replacing flooring, and they price accordingly. Third is hardware: helical piers, which screw in and suit lighter loads or tight spaces, usually sit above pressed steel piles in the same soil.

How much does foundation repair cost per pier?

Foundation repair cost per pier typically runs $1,000 to $2,500 installed, based on published national ranges. The price depends on depth to stable soil, pier type, pressed steel versus helical, and whether the pier goes inside or outside the house. Anyone quoting far below that band is usually excluding something, such as the bracket, the excavation or the lift, so compare the full line item rather than the headline number.

Why do foundation repair quotes vary so much for the same house?

Foundation repair quotes vary because companies propose different scopes, not just different prices. One quote may count piers from an elevation survey; another may wrap the whole perimeter by default. Pier type, depth assumptions and what is bundled in, drainage, permits, restoration, all move the total. Compare quotes line by line on pier count, per-pier price and warranty before judging any single total.

Cost by problem: what each symptom usually takes

Most homeowners do not shop by method; they shop by symptom. The table below works that way. Find the problem you can see, read across to the fix it usually needs, and you have a working budget range before anyone visits. Treat it as orientation, not a quote: the same stair-step crack can mean a $500 injection on a stable wall or the first visible sign of a corner that needs six piers, and only measurements separate the two.

Two patterns are worth remembering. First, water problems and structure problems often arrive together, and the honest fix sequences them: stabilise the structure, then handle drainage, or the water work gets torn up by the lift. Second, the cheapest line in the table, crack injection, is also the most oversold. Injection seals a crack; it does not hold up a house. If a contractor proposes injection for a wall that is bowing or a corner that is dropping, the low price is the warning, not the bargain.

Regional pricing moves the bands without breaking them. Labour rates in major metros push per-pier prices toward the top of the range, while deep or difficult soils, common in river deltas and expansive-clay country, add depth charges in any market. Frost-depth footings in cold states and high water tables in coastal ones both add work that a national average smooths away. This is why our city pages carry local cost notes, and why a quote at the top of a band is not automatically an unfair one. The fairness test in the next section is built to allow for exactly these local factors.

How many piers does a typical house need?

A typical house needs 4 to 6 piers for one settled corner, 8 to 14 for a full settled side, and 15 to 25 or more for whole-house settlement. Piers are usually spaced about 5 to 7 feet apart along the affected wall, so the length of settled wall drives the count. A quote far outside these patterns needs an elevation map to justify it.

Check your quote: the fairness test

The most common foundation question online is not really a pricing question. It is a homeowner holding a quote, asking strangers whether the number is fair. You can run that check yourself with four tests, using only the ranges and patterns on this page. Test one: divide the pier portion of the quote by the pier count. The result should sit inside $1,000 to $2,500 per pier installed. Test two: set the pier count against the settled length of wall, roughly one pier every 5 to 7 feet. A count wildly above that pattern needs elevation readings to explain it.

Test three: look for the measurements. A fair quote is built on an elevation survey and a pier diagram you get to keep; a quote built on a walk-around is an opinion with a price attached. Test four: read what is excluded. Landscaping restoration, interior floor removal, permits and cosmetic repairs are legitimately separate, but they should be listed as excluded, not discovered later. And when two quotes for the same house land far apart, the gap is almost always scope. Homeowners comparing offers routinely see totals tens of thousands of dollars apart, because one company priced the settled corner and the other priced the entire perimeter.

None of this makes you an engineer, and it is not meant to. It makes you a competent buyer. If a quote fails two or more of the tests, the next step is not an argument with the salesman; it is a second quote, or an independent evaluation, before anything is signed. FoundationMatch requires partner companies to quote itemised and in writing for exactly this reason: a quote that cannot be checked cannot be trusted, and a quote that can be checked usually survives the checking.

A final practical point: get the checked version in writing before the crew is booked, not after. Quotes get revised during scheduling, pier counts change when the first excavation opens, and verbal understandings from inspection day have a way of evaporating. A revised quote that re-states the per-pier price, the revised count and the reason for the change is normal and healthy. A revised total with no workings behind it is the red flag returning in a new costume. Keep every version; the paper trail is part of the repair.

  • No elevation survey behind the numbers, just a walk-around and a total
  • Pressure to sign today, or a discount that expires this week
  • A pier count far outside the typical patterns with no elevation map to justify it
  • One lump sum with no per-pier line item you can compare
  • Warranty promised verbally but not produced as a document before signing
  • A large deposit demanded before any materials arrive

What is a fair price per pier?

A fair price per pier is $1,000 to $2,500 installed, based on published national ranges. Within that band, deeper piers, helical piers and interior installations sit at the top; shallow exterior pressed piers sit at the bottom. A price below the band usually means excavation, brackets or the lift are billed separately. Ask what the per-pier price includes before comparing it to another quote.

Should I get a structural engineer before signing a big quote?

Yes, on any quote above roughly $10,000, an independent engineer is cheap insurance against the wrong scope. The evaluation costs about $350 to $600 and produces a stamped repair specification. Homeowners comparing contractor quotes often see totals tens of thousands of dollars apart for the same house; the engineer's specification makes every contractor price identical work, so the comparison finally means something.

The cost of waiting

Foundation movement rarely stays politely still. A corner that needs five piers this year may need eight in three years, and the secondary damage grows faster than the pier count: doors and windows rack until they need replacing rather than adjusting, supply lines and drains stress at the slab, and every cosmetic repair you do over a moving foundation, paint, drywall, tile, fails on schedule and has to be done twice. Sellers meet the sharpest version of this maths. A buyer pricing your house with a known, unrepaired problem discounts for the worst case, not the likely one, and the discount usually exceeds what the repair would have cost.

The honest counterweight matters too. Not every crack is active movement, and not every house needs piers this season. A hairline crack that has not changed across a full year of seasons, on a house with level floors and working doors, can be monitored for free with dated pencil marks and photographs. The cost of waiting applies to movement that is progressing. The way to know which one you have is measurement over time, or an evaluation that measures once, properly, instead of guessing either way.

There is also a middle path between ignoring the problem and funding the whole repair at once: fix the cause and the worst section first. Water management, grading and drainage work in the $2,000 to $6,000 range, or stabilising the one actively dropping corner, stops most of the compounding damage while you plan the rest. Ask any contractor quoting a full perimeter what they would do first with half the budget. The answer separates companies sequencing your repair from companies selling you a package.

Is it worth fixing the foundation of a house?

Yes, it is worth fixing a foundation when movement is active, because the damage compounds while the repair scope only grows. Stabilising early usually means fewer piers and less cosmetic damage to repair afterwards. A documented repair with a transferable warranty also removes the steep discount buyers apply to a house with known foundation problems. Monitoring is the right call only when movement has genuinely stopped.

Where these numbers come from: methodology

The ranges on this page were compiled in October 2026 from published national cost guides of the Angi, HomeAdvisor and HomeGuide class: large datasets built from reported project costs, contractor pricing and homeowner surveys. We publish bands rather than single averages because the bands are what survive checking against real quotes. The figures are reviewed quarterly, and city-level notes on our location pages flag where local conditions, deep soils, high water tables, difficult access, push real quotes toward the top of a band or beyond it.

One honest caveat: the published datasets overlap. The same underlying project data feeds several guides, so three websites agreeing on a range is not three independent witnesses. Treat every band here as orientation for checking a quote, never as a price promise. Your real number comes from measurements at your house, and FoundationMatch does not set contractor prices. What we can do is make sure the quote you receive is itemised enough to test against this page, line by line, before you sign it.

To use this page with a quote in hand, work in order. Find the method in the first table and check the unit price. Find your symptom in the second table and check the scope. Then run the four fairness tests on the document itself: unit price, pier-count pattern, measurements attached, exclusions listed. Ten minutes of that arithmetic will tell you more than an afternoon of reading reviews, and it turns the follow-up conversation with the contractor into specific questions with specific answers.

Does homeowners insurance cover foundation repair?

Homeowners insurance does not usually cover foundation repair for settlement, soil movement or wear. Those causes are excluded in most policies. Coverage sometimes applies when a sudden, accidental event, such as a burst pipe, damages the foundation. Our insurance and financing guide walks through what is covered, what is excluded, and how to document a claim if your damage came from a covered event.

Frequently asked questions

What is the cheapest foundation repair?

The cheapest foundation repair is monitoring a stable hairline crack, which costs nothing but attention and a dated pencil mark. The cheapest actual repair is crack injection, at $500 to $2,500. Be careful with bargain structural quotes: a pier job priced far below published ranges usually cuts the pier count, the depth, or the warranty, and an under-supported house tends to need the whole job redone later.

Can I negotiate a foundation repair quote?

Yes, foundation repair quotes are negotiable, and the cleanest leverage is a competing quote for the same measured scope. Ask each company to price the same pier count and layout, then compare per-pier prices, warranty terms and exclusions. Phasing the work or scheduling in a slower season can also move the total. Never negotiate by cutting piers below what the measurements support.

Why is my quote higher than the average foundation repair cost?

Your quote can exceed the average foundation repair cost for ordinary reasons: deeper soil before stable ground, interior piers instead of exterior ones, difficult access, or a second problem bundled in, such as drainage or crawl-space work. Averages blend small corner jobs with full perimeters. Ask the contractor to walk the line items and show which factors push your house above the typical band.

Does a foundation repair estimate cost anything?

A contractor's foundation repair estimate is usually free, because it is part of that company's sales process. An independent structural engineer's evaluation costs about $350 to $600 and is not free, because the report is the product and no repair sale follows from it. For large or conflicting scopes, the paid report is often the version worth trusting when you compare estimates.

How much does foundation repair cost for a two-storey house?

A two-storey house usually costs more to repair than a single-storey one of the same footprint, because wall loads are greater and pier spacing often tightens along the loaded walls. Expect totals at or above the $4,000 to $12,000 typical band for side or perimeter work. The real driver is still pier count and depth to stable soil, not the storey count alone, so the elevation survey decides the figure.

Should I repair the foundation before selling my house?

Repairing before selling usually beats discounting, because buyers and lenders price a known foundation problem at the worst case. A documented repair with a transferable warranty lets you sell at full market instead of negotiating against fear. Get the repair file in order first: contract, elevation reports and the warranty certificate. Our sellers' guide covers disclosure duties and the repair-versus-credit decision in full.
