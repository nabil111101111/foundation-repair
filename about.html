About FoundationMatch: a matching service, not a contractor

FoundationMatch connects homeowners who have foundation problems with local repair companies that meet published standards. We are not a foundation repair company. We own no trucks, employ no crews and hold no repair licence, because we never touch the work itself. A partner company inspects your home, writes the quote, does the repair and stands behind the warranty.

The service runs as a pilot in ten cities: Des Moines and Cedar Rapids, Baton Rouge, New Orleans and Shreveport, Lexington, Madison, Gulfport, Boise and Abilene. Each city page on this site is written for that market's soils, water conditions and housing stock, because a cracked slab in Abilene clay is a different problem from a bowed basement wall in Madison, and the advice should say so.

Everything else about how we operate is on this page and the two linked from it: how partners are chosen, how we make money, and how our cost figures are put together. We would rather show you the machinery than ask you to trust the brand.

How matching works, step by step

You start by describing what you are seeing: cracks, sticking doors, sloping floors, water in the basement, or a wall that is starting to bow. We also ask for your city and your foundation type if you know it. That is enough to route the request. We pass it to one partner company that covers your area and handles that kind of work, and the company contacts you to arrange an inspection at your home.

The inspection and the quote belong to that company, and so does every decision after it. You can accept the quote, reject it, or ask us for a second match where coverage allows. Nothing in the process obliges you to hire anyone, and you never pay FoundationMatch for the introduction. If a match goes badly, you can report it to us, and reports feed the standards review described below.

  • Step 1 — You describe the symptoms and your city
  • Step 2 — We match you with one vetted partner covering your area
  • Step 3 — The partner inspects your home and measures the movement
  • Step 4 — You receive a written, itemised quote you can compare line by line
  • Step 5 — You decide. Accept, reject, or ask for a second opinion

What happens after I describe my foundation problem?

After you send the form, we review your city, foundation type and symptoms, then pass your details to one vetted partner company that covers your area and does that kind of work. That company contacts you to arrange an inspection. You are not committed to anything at that point, and you can decline the match or ask us to withdraw your details.

Who actually does the repair work?

The repair work is done by the partner company you are matched with, not by FoundationMatch. FoundationMatch has no crews, no trucks and no repair licence, because we do not perform repairs. The contract, the warranty and the responsibility for the finished work all sit with the company that inspects your home and gives you the written quote.

Why this service exists: the pricing problem

FoundationMatch exists because of a specific frustration: foundation repair is one of the hardest home repairs to price from the outside. Most company websites publish no prices at all. The guides that do publish numbers often repeat a single average with no method behind it. So a homeowner with a cracked slab gets two or three quotes that differ by thousands of dollars, with no shared format to compare them in, and no way to tell whether the expensive quote is thorough or padded and the cheap one is efficient or corner-cutting.

The fix we can offer is not a magic price. It is structure. Published cost ranges with their sources shown, a standards page that tells you exactly what a quote should contain, and partner companies that agree in advance to quote in writing, line by line. A homeowner who can compare pier counts, per-pier prices and warranty terms across two quotes does not need us to tell them which company is honest. The paperwork shows it.

The worry behind the service is a pattern anyone can find in homeowner forums: a five-figure quote handed over after a twenty-minute visit, a pier count nobody explained, and the sinking feeling that the inspection was really a sales call. People in that position do not need another company telling them to relax. They need documents they can check. That is the gap a matching service can honestly fill, and it is the gap this one is built around.

Why do foundation repair prices vary so much between companies?

Foundation repair prices vary because no two quotes cover the same work. One company may price eight piers where another prices fourteen, one includes excavation and permits while another leaves them out, and one may add waterproofing the other never mentioned. Without an itemised quote, the totals are not comparable, which is exactly how homeowners end up overpaying.

Are the prices on this site quotes I can hold a company to?

No. The ranges on this site are published market ranges for planning and comparison, not quotes and not promises. Only a company that has inspected your home can give you a real price, and that price should arrive as a written, itemised quote. If a quote falls far outside the published range, ask which line items explain the difference.

How our cost data is compiled

Every cost figure on this site comes from published foundation repair pricing guides and cost datasets, compiled in October 2026. We work in ranges, not single averages, and we show the range next to the reasoning: piers are priced per pier installed, currently $1,000–$2,500 in the published guides, and a typical structural repair lands between $4,000 and $12,000 depending on how many piers the home needs and how hard they are to reach. An independent structural engineer's evaluation, for homeowners who want an opinion from someone who sells no repairs, runs about $350–$600 in the same published sources.

Two habits keep the data honest. First, each range carries its compilation date, because steel, labour and fuel costs move and a range without a date is a rumour. Second, we never present a published range as a local quote. Soil depth in Boise is not soil depth in New Orleans, and the city pages say what drives cost in that market instead of pretending one national number fits.

How current is your cost data?

Our cost ranges are compiled from published foundation repair pricing guides and cost data available in October 2026, and each range carries its compilation date. Foundation pricing shifts with labour, steel and fuel costs, so we review the ranges rather than treating them as permanent. If a range on this site ever looks stale, that date tells you how much to trust it.

Why do you publish ranges instead of exact prices?

We publish ranges because an exact price would be invented. Pier counts, soil depth, access and water problems change the cost of every job, and guides that print one average number hide all of that. A range with the reasoning behind it lets you sanity-check a quote: which end of the band your job should sit at, and why.

How partners are vetted before they can receive a match

A company cannot receive matches just by asking, and it cannot receive more of them by paying more. Before a company is listed, it has to evidence four things: a current licence in every state where licensing applies to this trade, liability insurance and workers' compensation, a commitment to written itemised quotes on every job, and warranty terms documented in plain language before work begins. The full checklist is published on our contractor standards page, written so a homeowner can run the same checks on any company, including companies that have nothing to do with us.

Standards only matter if they can be lost. A partner that refuses to quote in writing, stops honouring its documented warranty terms, or lets its licence or insurance lapse is removed from matching. Homeowner reports are part of that review. We would rather run a shorter list of companies that pass than a longer list that merely applied. The standards are the product. Everything else on this site is information.

What does a partner have to prove before joining?

Before joining, a partner company must show a current licence where its state requires one, carry liability insurance and workers' compensation, and agree to quote in writing with itemised line items. It must also document its warranty terms in plain language. These criteria are published on our contractor standards page, and companies that cannot evidence them are not listed.

What happens if a partner breaks the standards?

A partner that breaks the standards is removed from matching. Triggers include refusing to provide a written itemised quote, failing to honour documented warranty terms, or losing the licence or insurance the listing depends on. Homeowners can report a problem from any match, and repeated reports are reviewed against the published criteria rather than handled quietly behind the scenes.

What we will never do

Some promises are easy to make on an About page, so we keep this list short and checkable. Each item below is a policy you can test against anything else on this site.

  • Never publish a fake or bought review, rating or testimonial — pages carry no review scores until real, verifiable ones exist
  • Never rank or match companies by who pays the most — matching runs on coverage, job fit and the published standards
  • Never present ourselves as the contractor who will repair your home
  • Never invent a price, a statistic or a local fact to fill a page — where we do not know, the page says so
  • Never sell your details as a bulk lead list — your request goes to the company you were matched with

Will you ever list a company that pays more ahead of a better one?

No. Matching is based on coverage area, the kind of work a company does and whether it meets the published standards. Partners pay a fee for the matching service, described on our how-we-make-money page, but paying more does not buy a better position, because there is no ranked list to buy into. Homeowners see the company they were matched with, and why.

Frequently asked questions

Is FoundationMatch a foundation repair company?

No. FoundationMatch is a matching service that connects homeowners with vetted local foundation repair companies. We do not inspect homes, we do not sell piers and we do not carry out repairs. The company you are matched with does the inspection, writes the quote, performs the work and provides the warranty. Our job is the shortlist and the standards behind it.

Does it cost anything to use FoundationMatch?

No, homeowners pay nothing to use the service. You describe the problem, we match you with a vetted company, and that company arranges its own inspection and quote with you directly. FoundationMatch is paid by partner companies under the model explained on our how-we-make-money page. If anyone asks you for a fee in our name, do not pay it.

Which cities does FoundationMatch cover?

The pilot covers ten cities: Des Moines and Cedar Rapids in Iowa, Baton Rouge, New Orleans and Shreveport in Louisiana, Lexington in Kentucky, Madison in Wisconsin, Gulfport in Mississippi, Boise in Idaho and Abilene in Texas. Each city page is written for that market's soils and housing stock. If your city is not listed, the guides and glossary still apply to your home.

How do I know the company I am matched with is any good?

You do not have to take our word for it. Every partner must meet published standards covering licensing, insurance, written itemised quotes and documented warranty terms, and the full checklist sits on our contractor standards page. We also suggest you run the same checks yourself before signing, because a company that passes them has nothing to hide from a homeowner.

Can I get a second quote through FoundationMatch?

Yes, getting more than one quote is something we actively recommend. Foundation quotes for the same house can differ by thousands because pier counts and scopes differ, and the only way to see that is side by side. Tell us you want a second opinion and we will match you with another vetted company where coverage allows it.

Why should I trust a site that gets paid by repair companies?

You should not trust any site on faith, including this one. That is why the business model is published on its own page, the partner standards are public, and our cost ranges show their sources and compilation date. Everything a sceptical homeowner needs to check us is on the site. If a claim here cannot be verified, treat it as marketing and move on.
